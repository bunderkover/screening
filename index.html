Quiet Check · ADHD and autism screening · by underkover

Recognised ADHD and autism questionnaires, in one calm place.

A starting point for anyone wondering about ADHD or autism, for adults and children.

  • There are lots of online tests. Many ADHD and autism quizzes online have no research behind them, and it's hard to know which ones are real.
  • This site gathers recognised ones. Each questionnaire here comes from published research, with who built it, how accurate it is and its limits.
  • Nothing is recorded. Answers are scored in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent, so you stay anonymous.
  • Screening, not diagnosis. A proper assessment takes a clinician, time and care. This is a good starting point for that conversation.

Autism traits · adult 10-question self-check

AQ-10 Adult*

Self-report · 16+, no learning disability

A quick 10-question screen for autistic traits, recommended in NICE guideline CG142.

About this test →

About this test

Built by Allison, Auyeung and Baron-Cohen at the Cambridge Autism Research Centre (2012) as a quick 'red flag' to help decide on referral. In their volunteer sample: sensitivity 88%, specificity 91%. [Allison 2012] But in 476 adults referred to a UK diagnostic clinic, specificity was only 29%, and nearly two-thirds of people scoring below 6 were in fact autistic. Generalised anxiety may inflate scores. [Ashwood 2016]

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

Autism traits · adult full self-check

AQ-50*

Self-report · 16+, average or higher IQ

The full Autism-Spectrum Quotient. It measures autistic traits across social skill, attention switching, attention to detail, communication and imagination.

About this test →

About this test

Baron-Cohen et al., Cambridge, 2001. A trait measure: In the original study, most autistic adults scored much higher than controls. [Baron-Cohen 2001] In clinic referrals the AQ predicted diagnosis poorly (specificity 29%). [Ashwood 2016]

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

Autism traits · adult in-depth self-check

RAADS-R*

Clinician-administered self-report · 18+

An extended scale for autism traits in adults of average intelligence, covering social relatedness, language, sensory-motor and circumscribed interests.

About this test →

About this test

Ritvo et al., 2011, an international study of 779 adults at 9 centres. In that study it identified autistic adults with very high accuracy. [Ritvo 2011] The authors describe it as an aid to clinician diagnosis, not a stand-alone or online test. Results from people answering online on their own will differ.

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

About this test

Parent-report short form from the same 2012 Cambridge study (sensitivity 93%, specificity 95%), comparing teens recruited from an autism research database with controls. [Allison 2012] Accuracy in everyday or clinic settings is likely lower. A referral prompt, not a diagnosis.

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

ENFR

Autism early signs · toddler, 16–30 months, parent questionnaire

M-CHAT-R/F*

Parent report · 16–30 months

Screens toddlers for early signs of autism such as pointing, joint attention and response to their name.

About this test

Robins et al., 2014, with 16,071 toddlers screened at 18- and 24-month check-ups in the US. Of children positive after the follow-up interview, 47.5% were diagnosed with autism and 94.6% had some developmental delay or concern. [Robins 2014] The follow-up interview is an essential part of the test. Sensitivity is uncertain (estimated upper bound 85%).

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

SNAP-IV*

Parent or teacher · school-age (about 6–17)

Rates the 18 DSM ADHD symptoms (inattention, hyperactivity/impulsivity) plus 8 oppositional-defiance items.

About this test →

About this test

Swanson, Nolan and Pelham. The 26-item version rates the DSM ADHD and oppositional symptoms. In 250 referred children aged 6–17 it was sensitive but had low specificity against clinician diagnosis, so it's best used as a screen or outcome measure. [Hall 2019]

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

Vanderbilt 1st ed*

Parent report · 6–12 (teacher form: 43 items)

Screens for childhood ADHD symptoms plus oppositional, conduct and anxiety/depression symptoms, and how the child is doing at home and school.

About this test →

About this test

NICHQ Vanderbilt scales (Wolraich et al.). In 243 referred children the parent scale was internally consistent and matched a diagnostic interview fairly well (r = .79). [Wolraich 2003] The AAP notes it was validated for ages 6–12 and should not be used alone to diagnose. [AAP]

A score is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician's full assessment can diagnose.

Before you begin

These questionnaires are screening tools only and cannot provide a diagnosis. If any result concerns you, or if you have concerns regardless of the result, please speak with your GP (médecin traitant) or a qualified clinician. In an emergency in France call 15 or 112.
